Research

Shen Lab develops reproducible computational methods and contributes to longitudinal human studies, connecting metabolomics, the microbiome, other omics and wearable data. Our work spans tool building, biological interpretation and the study of health over time.

From mass spectra to metabolic function

How can we make metabolomics reproducible and biologically interpretable?

We develop open computational tools for LC–MS data processing, compound annotation and functional analysis. TidyMass provides a modular, traceable workflow; metID automates compound annotation; TidyMass2 extends analysis to metabolite origins and functional modules, including information carried by unannotated features.

Selected publications

  • TidyMass an object-oriented reproducible analysis framework for LC–MS dataNature Communications
  • TidyMass2 Advancing LC-MS Untargeted Metabolomics Through Metabolite Origin Inference and Metabolic Feature-based Functional Module AnalysisNature Communications
  • metID: an R package for automatable compound annotation for LC− MS-based dataBioinformatics

AI and networks for multi-omics interpretation

Which molecules act together, and how do their relationships change over time?

We combine biological networks, statistical modelling and AI to turn multi-omics measurements into testable hypotheses. MAPA discovers functional modules and uses literature-grounded language models to interpret them, with applications to aging-related datasets. lagCI examines time-lagged relationships in dense molecular and wearable time series. These inferred relationships guide follow-up experiments rather than establishing causality on their own.

Selected publications

  • MAPA: A Semantic Network Framework for Functional Module Discovery and Interpretation in Multi‐Omics DataAdvanced Science
  • LagCI Enables Inference of Temporal Causal Relationships from Dense Multi-Omic Time SeriesBriefings in Bioinformatics

Microbial communities and host metabolism

How do microbial communities across the body relate to human physiology?

Our collaborative studies connect microbial dynamics with host molecular and clinical measurements. Longitudinal profiling across four body sites reveals both stable, individual-specific communities and changes associated with health and disease. Related work examines cross-body-site microbial interactions and their links to the circulating metabolome.

Selected publications

  • Longitudinal profiling of the microbiome at four body sites reveals core stability and individualized dynamics during health and diseaseCell Host Microbe
  • Cross-body site microbial interactions influence the human plasma metabolomeMicrobiome

Measuring health as a trajectory

What can repeated molecular measurements reveal beyond a single snapshot?

We study how molecular profiles vary with daily life and environmental exposure. Multi-omics microsampling supports frequent measurements of lifestyle-associated changes, while longitudinal exposome profiling links external exposures with internal molecular responses. Together with wearable data, these approaches motivate personalized monitoring that accounts for each person’s baseline and variability.

Selected publications

  • Multi-omics microsampling for the profiling of lifestyle-associated changes in healthNature Biomedical Engineering
  • Precision environmental health monitoring by longitudinal exposome and multi-omics profilingGenome Research

Metabolic trajectories of pregnancy

Can metabolic changes help characterize gestational progression?

Our pregnancy studies characterize coordinated metabolic changes in longitudinal blood and urine samples. We develop and evaluate models of gestational age and time to delivery, and examine pathways that change across pregnancy. The aim is to build a molecular understanding of maternal physiology and a foundation for future biomarker validation.

Selected publications

  • Metabolic dynamics and prediction of gestational age and time to delivery in pregnant womenCell
  • Longitudinal urine metabolic profiling and gestational age prediction in human pregnancyBriefings in Bioinformatics
